# When your syllabus changes

**When a board publishes a new syllabus, Wobo tells you what moved and asks whether you want to switch. Nothing changes underneath you.**

**How it works**

Each academic year or edition is a separate version. You are pinned to the one you chose, and it never changes after you have started on it. A correction is a new version too, never an edit to the one you are using.

**When a new one arrives**

We check the boards' own pages regularly and around the times they usually publish. If the document has changed, Wobo builds the new version, checks it the same way as the first, and then tells you, in one line per change:

- Chapters that were added or removed.
- Chapters that moved to a different class.
- Topics that were renamed.

**Switching**

You decide. Switch and your progress carries across, matched chapter by chapter; anything that no longer exists is kept in your history rather than deleted. Your own edits are reapplied, and Wobo tells you if any of them no longer has a home.

**Staying**

Staying on your version is a perfectly good answer, especially mid-year. Wobo will not ask again until the next one.

**Halfway through the year**

If a change lands mid-year, Wobo mentions it once, quietly, and does not push. Your exam is on the syllabus your school is teaching, not on the newest document.
