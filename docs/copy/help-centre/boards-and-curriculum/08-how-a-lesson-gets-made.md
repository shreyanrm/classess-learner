# How a lesson gets made

**Nothing is made in advance. When you open a topic, Wobo builds that lesson for you, checks it, and draws it.**

**Why it works that way**

A lesson written once for everybody is written for nobody. Built at the moment you open it, a lesson can use your board's wording, your class's depth, the examples you like, and the things you already know as scaffolding.

**What Wobo does before drawing**

- Works out the shape of the explanation for this topic and this learner.
- Computes every number with code. Equations are solved, units are checked, chemical equations are balanced.
- Has the whole thing checked independently before you see it. Anything that fails is redrawn once, and if it fails again Wobo refuses rather than serving it.

That is why Wobo will occasionally say "I could not get this right, give me a moment" instead of showing you something plausible.

**Speed**

The first time anyone opens a topic it takes a few seconds to build. After that it is much faster for everyone, because the teaching for the same idea is reused and re-fitted to your board and your class.

**Different every time**

Ask again tomorrow with a different question and you get a different explanation, not the same page. The topic is the same; the teaching is for the moment you asked.

**If something is wrong anyway**

Flag it. Checking is good, not perfect, and you are the last check.
